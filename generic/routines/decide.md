# Decide

Use it with no argument (the work in this session) or with a topic, for example "should we move billing to annual plans".

Stop and help me decide. Do not build anything yet. Interview me about the decisions that are mine to make.

## 1. Find the decisions

Read the topic if one is given. Otherwise read the work in this session so far. List every choice that needs an answer.

Sort them into two piles:

- **Mine to make.** Scope, priorities, money, risk I am willing to take, anything hard to undo, anything customers or other people will see, and any real fork where two good answers exist.
- **Yours to settle.** Anything you can answer from the code, the docs or a sensible default. Do not ask me about these. Decide them, and list each in one line at the end so I can overrule you.

If nothing needs my input, say so in one sentence and stop.

## 2. Ask, a few at a time

Ask the decision that blocks the others first. Ask at most four questions in a round. If an answer opens new decisions, ask them in the next round.

Make every question stand on its own. I may see only the question, not your earlier text. Say what the choice is and why it matters in one or two sentences.

For each question:

- Give two to four real options. No filler options.
- Put your recommendation first and mark it "(Recommended)".
- For each option, say in plain words what it means, what it costs or risks, and what it gives me. Include time, money and how easy it is to undo where they matter.
- Say why you recommend your pick, and when a different option would be better.
- Name the main trade-off. If I pick the recommended option, what do I give up?

## 3. Write in plain English

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- No jargon. If a technical word is needed, explain it in a few words the first time.
- Say what will happen, not how it works inside.
- Be direct. Give a recommendation, not a survey. Do not hedge.
- Do not invent numbers, costs or dates. If you do not know a figure, say so.

## 4. After my answers

1. Play back what I decided as a short list: "You decided: ...".
2. If I went against your recommendation, say once what the main consequence is. Do not argue. It is my call.
3. If my answers raise new questions, ask them. Do not ask me again about anything already decided.
4. List the decisions you settled yourself, one line each.
5. Then ask whether to go ahead, or carry on with the work if I already said to. Do only what the decisions cover.

## Decisions that are yours to edit

Add the things that are always mine to make in your organization. For example: prices and plans, anything customers see, turning a flag on in production, applying a migration, changing which model serves a task, spending on live tests, closing an issue.

## Rules

- Never decide for me anything in the "mine to make" pile.
- Never ask a question just to confirm the obvious.
- Do not stack more than four questions in one round.
- If I say "you decide", pick your recommended option, say so, and move on.
